Contents · LUT reinforcement-learning actor → spikes (int4, live)

A real Walker2d SAC control policy (32 lookup tables over the 17-D observation) compiled into a latency-coded spiking network, running live in your browser: each observation becomes input-neuron spike times, the 4-bit integer weights make the comparison Σ w·t<θ exact, and the 6 actions come out as output-neuron spike times — decoded and checked against the lookup-table oracle below. Pick an example and watch it run.

Cyan dots (top) = the 17 observation inputs, each a single spike whose TIME encodes its value (earlier = larger). The dashed line marks where all 32 tables' 6-bit addresses are resolved. Purple dots (bottom) = the 6 action outputs, each a spike whose TIME encodes the action value; the number is the decoded action. The playhead sweeps the 256-tick window.

Does the spiking output match the trained policy?

"LUT oracle" = the 4-bit lookup-table actor evaluated in float on the raw observation. "spiking" = the action decoded from the output-neuron spike time. They match to within the output-spike timing quantization; the only source of any mismatch is a rare boundary address-flip from binning the continuous observation onto the tick grid — the integer-weight comparison itself is exact.

Why this matters

It closes the loop from our HLUT→spiking conversion ladder to a REAL reinforcement-learning controller: a trained Walker2d policy transfers to a spiking network with negligible action error at a modest tick budget. Because the weights are 4-bit integers, the weighted-timing comparison is exact — no analog tick budget is needed for the decision, only for representing the input observation at the desired resolution. Everything here runs client-side from a 59 KB embedded model; nothing is precomputed.
