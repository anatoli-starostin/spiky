# Regularisation

Short notes on the regularisers we tried for LUTs: dropout of whole tables, total-variation penalties on the table rows (a highlighted technique), weight decay and penalties, and a list of things that did not help. Each technique is one short subsection with a few sentences and links. No evaluation numbers are given here (the evidence is summarised in short paragraphs that link to the [Architectures](../architectures/README.md) pages). Many results are single seeds, many of the language-model runs at `e85a5ce` use the old evaluation (see [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../../CONVENTIONS.md)), and many were killed early. Whether a regulariser helps depends strongly on the data regime: on a small looped corpus (several passes over 4 shards) regularisation helps, while on the full corpus under one epoch the models are data bound and it does not.

## 1. Dropout

### 1.1 Whole-table dropout

Table dropout works in the multi-pass regime (one seed each) and is unknown under one epoch. For every input, head and table a Bernoulli draw drops the whole table in training, the surviving tables are rescaled, and evaluation reads all tables. On the corpus of 4 shards looped several times, a modest rate improved every arm tried, including the best quantised cell, and shrank the gap between train and validation loss. In the d24 nanochat run, switching it off for the first 1500 steps changed nothing measurable, and a dense dropout control hurt under one epoch ([FFN replacement, section 5](../architectures/transformers/ffn-replacement.md#5-lutorch_ex-cartridges-on-the-full-corpus-october-2026)). So the effect looks like regularisation against overfitting over several passes. There is no run that isolates head dropout at other rates, the 4-shard regime is flagged as an invalid comparison regime for the `abl47` family, and whether it helps at the end of the 5568-step d24 run is unknown. The nanochat README sentence on memory reads backwards against its own table. [bpb figures 4747 README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/lut_ablation/bpb_figures_4747/README.md), [Light head dropout](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/src/spiky/lutorch/light_multi_head_lut.py#L971-L984), [test_light_head_dropout](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/src/spiky/lutorch/tests/test_light_head_dropout.py), [nodropout1500 README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/results/d24-lutffn-confn1-1xh100-s0-nodropout1500/README.md), [lutorch_ex README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/src/spiky/lutorch_ex/README.md#L430-L433).

## 2. Total-variation penalties

Total-variation (TV) penalties are a highlighted technique here. A table has $2^n$ rows $W[\ell]$ indexed by an address $\ell$ of $n$ bits, so the rows are the vertices of a hypercube. The cell TV penalty is the mean squared distance between rows whose addresses differ in one bit,

$$\mathrm{TV}=\frac{1}{T\cdot n\cdot 2^{n-1}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{\ell_j=0}\big\lVert W_t[\ell]-W_t[\ell\oplus e_j]\big\rVert^2$$

where $T$ is the number of tables, $e_j$ flips bit $j$ and $\oplus$ is exclusive or. Reading: neighbouring rows of a table are pulled towards each other, so a wrong neighbour read after an address flip costs little. The normalisation by the number of pairs is taken from the code ([`cell_tv`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/src/spiky/lutorch/light_multi_head_lut.py#L788-L807)). The penalty is added as a separate backward once per step before clipping, in training only. It exists in [`lutorch_ex`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/src/spiky/lutorch_ex/cartridges/manifesto_base.py#L264-L286) and in the older modules; the main-branch [`FastMultiHeadLut`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/fast_multi_head_lut.py#L749-L943) has no TV.

### 2.1 Cell total-variation penalty

The TV penalty gave a consistent small gain, one seed each. In the 21-cell ablation every paired comparison improved with the penalty, and a sweep of its weight improved steadily up to the value used in all runs, which still led at 48k steps. The d24 nanochat runs use it throughout but never ablated it. All of this is on the looped 4-shard corpus, the regime where regularisation helps; in the fused 170-shard branch TV is on in every arm, and the twin without TV of the quantised 48k arm has empty metrics. [lut_ablation table v4](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/doc/research/lut_ablation/lut_ablation_table_v4.tex#L274-L281), [test_fast_mhl_cell_tv](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/src/spiky/lutorch/tests/test_fast_mhl_cell_tv.py), [TV dispatch tests](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/src/spiky/lutorch_ex/tests/test_cell_tv_dispatch.py), [manifesto_base cell_tv](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/src/spiky/lutorch_ex/cartridges/manifesto_base.py#L264-L286), [48k TV pair](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs_corrected/exp_n_0238_pureLUT0193_TVlam10_48k_seed1).

### 2.2 TV schedules and a Kohonen kernel

Variants of the TV penalty did not help, with differences of a few thousandths and no sweep of the kernel. Annealing the TV weight to zero was slightly worse than a constant weight, and a late ramp matched the control, so TV acts through early and middle training. A graded Kohonen-style penalty, which weights pairs of rows by a Gaussian of their Hamming distance with an annealed width, is the radius-one TV in its narrow limit; at the settings tried it was weaker than flat TV. [anneal config](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/lut_ablation/exp_n_abl_47_B48k_light_lmfrozeng_n2_tau0p5_tvanneal10to0_tph64_h8_seed1_p2int8/config.json), [late ramp train.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs_corrected/exp_n_0240_pureLUT0193_TVlam10_lateramp_48k_seed1/train.py#L140-L160), [Kohonen code](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/src/spiky/lutorch/light_multi_head_lut.py#L809-L840).

## 3. Weight decay and penalties

### 3.1 Weight decay on the tables

Our recipe is no weight decay on tables, and the effect is neutral. Tables, temperatures, biases and norms get no weight decay, and the dense matrices get a decoupled one. Exempting the tables was harmless and not a measurable win. An analysis argues that decay on table weights is redundant because the output magnitude is normalised away downstream, so decay would only act as a learning rate. Decay on the unembedder matters, because it sets the logit temperature, and was not tested. The report table credits the whole gain of [exp291](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp291_no_wd_on_luts) to no decay, but [exp291](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp291_no_wd_on_luts) was forked from a run that also changed the stream initialisation, and no sweep with non-zero decay on tables exists. [exp191](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp191_lut_no_wd), [exp291](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp291_no_wd_on_luts), [exp303 report table](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/exp303_short_report.tex#L100-L125), [lut_ablation README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/lut_ablation/README.md#L81), [run train.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs/exp_n_0127_grid_H4d48_nap7_tph128/train.py#L193-L209).

### 3.2 A sine penalty pulling weights to a quantisation grid

The grid penalty was neutral on the loss and snapped the weights to the grid, in a byte-level series with one run and no matched run without the penalty in the note that first describes it. A penalty on the squared sine of the weight times the grid size, ramped late in training, pulls the weights to a coarse grid. Evaluation with snapped weights was as good as without, and the loss matched the unregularised run, so low-bit storage cost nothing in that setting. A separate int8 quantisation-aware run with always-quantised weights and quantised gradients stuck far behind. [exp146](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp146_quant_reg_100k), [exp145](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp145_quant_reg), [exp147](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp147_int8_qat).

## 4. Other things we tried

Each paragraph says what was done and what happened. None of these helped; most are single runs, and several were killed early.

### 4.1 Gaussian noise on table inputs

Input noise did not help, and the runs were killed early. Gaussian noise on all table inputs lost clearly at two scales, so it was dropped. [exp385](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp385_input_noise_0p01).

### 4.2 `output_scale_noise` and `input_scale_noise`

Both noise options did not help, with one run each in the byte-level series. In training, `output_scale_noise` of [`MultiHeadLut`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/multi_head_lut.py#L161-L485) multiplies each sample and head of the output by an independent random factor between one minus and one plus the noise level, and `input_scale_noise` does the same to the input. On the smooth routing model both were worse than no noise, also with three alternatives per table and for the rank-attention version. They are the scale-side relatives of the input noise of 4.1; the layers are described in [Normalisation 2](normalisation.md#2-output-side-and-weight-side-normalisation). [exp027](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp027_smooth_output_scale_noise), [exp029](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp029_smooth_input_scale_noise), [early series notes](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/transformer_exps/experiments.md#L453-L486).

### 4.3 Structural auxiliary regularisers on tables

None of these helped, the runs were killed early, and one run ([exp245](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp245_gated_tables)) gave no result. A load-balancing loss on the row selections, a cosine penalty between tables (dormant, because tables start nearly orthogonal), a regulariser forcing large differences between anchors, an uncertainty bias on the smooth interpolation, a pairwise contrastive decorrelation of rows and an adaptive per-table dropout were each neutral or worse. [exp383](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp383_load_balance_lam0p01), [exp361](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp361_inter_table_contrast), [exp026](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp026_smooth_delta_reg), [exp246](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp246_adaptive_dropout), [soft wgrad and contrast note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_soft_wgrad_and_contrast_dead_ends.md).

### 4.4 Reconstruction auxiliary

The mirror loss gave at best a marginal gain in a narrow regime (0.0009 against its matched control, one seed). During training a mirror block maps the output of a feed-forward slot back to its input, and its error is added with a small weight; the mirrors are dropped at inference. The small weight gave a small gain, a larger weight a loss. It does not close the gap to the dense model and costs a lot of training time. [exp_n_0051](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0051_H8_d48_tph64_recon_16k/README.md), [exp_n_0053](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0053_H8_d48_tph64_recon_lam0p5_16k/README.md).
