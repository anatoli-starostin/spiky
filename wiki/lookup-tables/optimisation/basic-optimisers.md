# Basic optimisers applied to LUTs

Short notes on the standard optimisers we tried on LUT parameters (SGD, Adam, Lion, Muon) and what they do on table rows. Each technique is one short subsection with a few sentences and links. Optimisers built for the LUT structure (per-row, per-cell, bit latents) are on [Special LUT optimisers](special-optimisers.md); learning rates, schedules and precision are on [Training efficiency](training-efficiency.md). No evaluation numbers are given here (the evidence is summarised in short paragraphs that link to the [Architectures](../architectures/README.md) pages); most nanochat runs at `e85a5ce` are single seeds with the old evaluation (see [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../../CONVENTIONS.md)), and many were killed early.

## 1. AdamW, Lion and the sign family

### 1.1 AdamW as the default recipe

We use AdamW as the default, inherited from the dense baseline and never ablated piece by piece. Nearly every LUT language-model run uses it with the usual betas and decoupled weight decay on the dense matrices. The table tensors and all one-dimensional parameters go to a group without decay, and there is one gradient clip and a warmup then decay schedule (schedules are in [Training efficiency 2.1](training-efficiency.md#21-schedule-and-warmup)). In the nanochat runs the tables are split off into their own group with their own learning rate and betas. A footnote of the ffn_replacement paper says that the recipe is tuned for dense models and LUT models may prefer other optimisers; this was not tested. [fused-cartridge recipe](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b1a5a7131732f93595f53bf572299139eb1a0856/experiments/lutorch_ex_fused/README.md#shared-geometry--settings-identical-in-all-8-lut-configjson-00_vanilla-shares-the-backbonerecipe-no-lut-keys), [nanochat experiments overview](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/EXPERIMENTS.md#standard-hyperparameters-unless-noted), [group routing in gpt.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/nanochat/nanochat/gpt.py#L419-L491).

### 1.2 Lion on the table group

Lion on the tables works as a memory saving, but it was not shown to improve quality, and the runs are single seeds. Only the table tensors were moved to Lion, while embeddings, head, norms and temperatures stay on AdamW. With the second beta near 0.95 Lion matched AdamW within seed noise at half the optimiser state and became the default table optimiser of the later nanochat runs and of the shipped [`examples/lutgpt`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/examples/lutgpt) recipe. With the default second beta near 0.99 it was clearly worse (stale momentum signs on rarely visited rows), and its learning rate matters because every coordinate moves by exactly the learning rate. In the first hyperplane pass Lion was marginally better in one short run, and the later baselines went back to AdamW without a clear loss, so the two eras do not agree on more than the memory saving. [optimiser sweep note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_optimizer_sweep.md), [optimiser sweep memo](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/lut_lm_optsweep.tex), [exp453](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp453_lion_b2_0p95), [lutgpt recipe](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/examples/lutgpt/train.py#L96-L112), [hyperplane exp072](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp072_compressionmhl_6heads_inner64_lionlut_4k/README.md).

### 1.3 Signum ties Adam

Signum worked in our killed runs, and the reading of why is only an idea. The sweep ordered updates by how much gradient magnitude they keep: SGD with momentum keeps all of it, signSGD with momentum and Lion keep only the sign, and AdamW normalises per coordinate. Signum came within noise of AdamW at half the state, and Lion with both betas equal is Signum ([exp452](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp452_lion_b2_0p9) reproduced [exp448](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp448_signsgd_lut)). The reading drawn in the notes is that table rows are limited by gradient noise on sparse rows, not by conditioning, so methods that discard magnitude do well; this is an interpretation, not a test. [optimiser sweep note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_optimizer_sweep.md), [summary 2026-05-20](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/summary_2026-05-20.md), [exp448](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp448_signsgd_lut).

### 1.4 SGD

The two eras contradict each other on SGD, and it is understudied and to be revisited. The early era used a lot of SGD: in the byte-level series SGD with momentum was marginally better than Adam in two comparisons, and the old [`lut_fused`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lut_fused) trainer used plain SGD with a decaying rate for the LUT layers. The Manifesto itself shows LUT architectures that train well with SGD. On the nanochat tables SGD with momentum needed a learning rate read from the measured gradient scale (a small rate stalls) and still ended behind AdamW, with a large catch-up during the decay. The early comparison predates the dual-stream recipe and was never repeated. [optimiser sweep note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_optimizer_sweep.md), [exp451](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp451_sgd_mom_lr0p1), [early series SUMMARY](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/transformer_exps/SUMMARY.md), [legacy `lut_fused` trainer](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/workbooks/lutorch_transformer.py#L256-L269).

### 1.5 Momentum and betas

Beta1 near 0.9 and the Lion second beta near 0.95 work well in our runs, on short proxies and single seeds. Setting the first beta to zero on the tables was worse at every batch size tried and much worse at a large batch, so momentum acts as a free integrator over sparse row visits. A sweep of the first beta showed a flat plateau and a permanent loss for very large values, because the average cannot follow the warmup ramp; on the hyperplane line the loss grew steadily with it. Lowering the second Adam beta to 0.95 for the bit-LUT optimiser helped slightly. A sparse-aware fix proposed for the first-beta deficit did not work (see [Special LUT optimisers 3.1](special-optimisers.md#31-sparse-row-aware-adam-variants-and-per-row-learning-rates)). [beta1 ablation](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_beta1_ablation.md), [beta1 plateau](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_beta1_plateau.md), [beta1 at large batch](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_beta1_load_bearing_at_bs128.md), [hyperplane exp_n_0074](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0074_table_momentum/README.md), [exp020](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp020_beta2_0p95).

## 2. Muon

We started to study Muon in the context of the full-scale LUT NanoChat experiments, but it is still unclear how to adapt it to LUT tables. Dense Muon on the transformer matrices stayed ahead of AdamW in the dense d24 baseline (one seed per optimiser, and the AdamW rate was not swept). A table read is linear in the cell vector, so there is nothing to orthogonalise across cells; the one adaptation we tried, [`lut_whiten_step`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/nanochat/nanochat/optim.py#L183-L216) (whitening of the width dimension per head, then a row normalisation of each cell), was run once, for 500 and 1000 steps with very little effort, and did not help. It is to be revisited. [dense Muon summary](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/results/d24-dense-1xh100-s0/summary.json#L3-L8), [whiten500 README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/results/d24-lutffn-confn1-1xh100-s0-whiten500/README.md).

## 3. Other things we tried

Each paragraph says what was done and what happened. None of these helped; all are single settings, and the runs were killed early. The finding that a penalty weight has no effect under Adam is on [Ternary routing 3.2](../lookup-table-primitives/ternary.md#32-a-penalty-weight-has-no-effect-under-adam).

### 3.1 Lookahead on the table group

We tried one setting, killed at step 2200, and it did not help. Lookahead (every 5 steps, slow weights moved halfway to the fast weights, Adam state not reset) was added on the table group only and otherwise matched to an AdamW run at the same learning rate. It trailed that run at every evaluation by a small margin that was never positive. The explanation in the note is double smoothing: AdamW with the first beta at 0.9 already averages gradients, Lookahead adds a weight average on top, and the periodic reset throws away steps that momentum had already pointed in a good direction. A larger interval or a smaller mixing weight were not tried, and the note considers a combination with the first beta at zero a long shot, because zero alone was already worse. [note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lookahead_neutral_tiny.md), [exp357](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp357_lookahead_k5_a05).

### 3.2 Windowed gradient averaging

We tried two runs, killed at about step 1200, and it did not help. The idea was to get the denser per-row statistics of a large batch at the cost of a small one: keep a ring buffer of the last 8 table gradients and use their mean as the gradient before the Adam step (a buffer of the size of the table group, in fp32). On top of momentum it was worse at every step, again from double smoothing, with stale gradients computed against older row values. Replacing momentum with it matched the baseline for the first 800 steps, then spiked once the learning rate peaked and the run became unstable. The note closes this direction of gradient variance reduction at a fixed physical batch; real gradient accumulation (see [Training efficiency 1.1](training-efficiency.md#11-batch-size-is-the-biggest-lever-and-accumulation-reproduces-it)) is the working way. [note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_windowed_grad_dead_end.md), [exp368](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp368_windowed_grad_w8).

### 3.3 EGGROLL, a gradient-free optimiser

We tried this in the early byte-level series, in two runs with the second killed early, and it did not help. EGGROLL is an evolution strategy with antithetic rank-one perturbations of all trainable tensors (tables, embeddings, unembedder), with no backward pass. Run from scratch on a small byte-level LUT transformer (8 perturbation pairs, small noise) it learned only slowly and flattened at a high loss, far above the Adam run it was forked from. The second run trained with Adam for 10k steps and then switched to EGGROLL with a constant large step; right after the switch the loss rose and did not recover before the run was stopped. The `summary.json` files of both folders are copies of the parent run, so only the metrics tables were used here. [exp137](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp137_eggroll), [exp138](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp138_adam_then_eggroll).
