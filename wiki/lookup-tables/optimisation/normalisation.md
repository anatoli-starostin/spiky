# Normalisation

Short notes on the normalisations we tried around LUTs: standard LayerNorm (which works) with QK norm as an example, the norm in front of the tables, and a handful of output-side and weight-side normalisations that were tried once. Each technique is one short subsection with a few sentences and links. No evaluation numbers are given here (the evidence is summarised in short paragraphs that link to the [Architectures](../architectures/README.md) pages); many nanochat runs at `e85a5ce` are single seeds with the old evaluation (see [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../CONVENTIONS.md)), and many were killed early, so "equal" means equal along the trajectory at the same step.

## 1. LayerNorm and the norm before the tables

### 1.1 Standard LayerNorm

A standard LayerNorm after a LUT block works nicely and helps in many of our architectures, in one byte-level series and in single-seed nanochat runs. Table outputs have erratic magnitudes, and a LayerNorm brings them back to a scale the next layer can use. For an input $x$ with $d$ entries it is, with a small constant $\varepsilon$ and the optional learnable gain $\gamma_i$ and shift $\beta_i$:

$$\mu=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_j,\qquad \sigma^2=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}(x_j-\mu)^2,\qquad y_i=\gamma_i \frac{x_i-\mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\varepsilon}}+\beta_i.$$

It centres the entries, divides by their standard deviation and then rescales them. Before the gain and shift, every entry is divided by the same scale, so the order of the entries within one input does not change. A norm only after the output projection was best, dropping the attention-output norm helped, and dropping all norms was worse (dead weights and collapsed activations). [exp152](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp152_ln_out_proj_only), [exp187](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp187_no_attn_norm), [exp202](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp202_lut_lnpre).

### 1.2 QK norm as an example

QK norm is one use of the same LayerNorm: it normalises the queries and keys, which come from table outputs, before the dot product. In the byte-level series it was what allowed the table capacity to scale. On the nanochat line the raw queries and keys into the attention function with a QK norm beat a learnable attention scale, and removing the QK norm made training bad. [SUMMARY, phase 13](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/SUMMARY.md#L411), [exp220](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp220_qk_norm), [exp223](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp223_qk_init_001_qknorm).

### 1.3 The norm before the tables only calibrates magnitude

The norm that feeds the tables works, judged by trajectory comparison ([exp470](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp470_centerscale_norm) to [exp474](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp474_rmsnorm_nocenter) were killed early and only [exp475](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp475_meanabs_nocenter) finished). A two by two over L1 or L2 and centred or not, plus ablations of the affine part, on the norm that feeds the tables showed that it does one thing: it keeps the absolute differences between anchors near the soft temperature, so the softmax of the soft backward is neither uniform nor saturated (the gradient side is on [Gradient backpropagation](../lookup-table-primitives/backpropagation.md#31-all-k-rows-pinned-to-the-chosen-row)). Without any divisor the gradient is diffuse and training is clearly worse. The learnable gain and shift are inert, and any positive scale per input (standard deviation, RMS, mean absolute deviation, mean absolute value) is equivalent. It is an effect on training dynamics only: the divisor keeps signs and never changes which row is picked. It was not tested whether `lutorch_ex` models still need it. [note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_prenorm_magnitude_calibration.md), [summary 2026-05-21](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/summary_2026-05-21.md), [exp472](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp472_std_only_noaffine).

### 1.4 MeanAbsNorm

We use this normalisation, which is a cost win and a quality tie. The input $x$ with $d$ entries is divided by its mean absolute value plus a small constant $\varepsilon$ as follows:

$$y_i=\frac{x_i}{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lvert x_j\rvert+\varepsilon}$$

Reading: an L1 norm with no centring, no affine part, no square and no root, so no parameters. It equals the LayerNorm baseline within noise, is the cheapest valid norm before a table and the friendliest to matmul-free hardware, and became the baseline recipe; later notes use its scale invariance to decouple the scale of a tied embedding from the table input. On the hyperplane backbone a comparison with LayerNorm was stopped at step 2,800 of 16,000 without a verdict, and a swap from MeanAbsNorm to LayerNorm in the LUT-GPT line was started and stopped with LayerNorm slightly behind. The name MeanAbsNorm appears in later notes. [note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_prenorm_magnitude_calibration.md), [exp475](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp475_meanabs_nocenter), [hyperplane exp018](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp018_layernorm_vs_meanabsnorm/README.md), [journey note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/claude/experiment-journey.md#L61-L63).

## 2. Output-side and weight-side normalisation

Two options of the older [`lutorch`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch) layers change the scale of a table output or of its projection weights, not which row is read: `calibrate_output` in [`MultiHeadLut`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/multi_head_lut.py#L161-L485), and `normalize_weights` in [`LProjection`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/l_projection.py#L248-L443) (code at `e85a5ce`). They are not in `lutorch_ex`, and each was tried at most once. The scale noises of the same layer are on [Regularisation 4.2](regularisation.md#42-output_scale_noise-and-input_scale_noise).

### 2.1 `calibrate_output`

No run enables this readout normalisation, so its effect is unknown. In `MultiHeadLut` ([`multi_head_lut.py`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/multi_head_lut.py#L148-L158)) the sum over tables of each sample and head is divided by its range over the output features, the maximum minus the minimum, which makes the readout scale free. Every config in both experiment folders has it off, the fast attention path bypasses it and the tests pin it off. The only related measured finding is the pre-table calibration of 1.2. [forward use](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/multi_head_lut.py#L476-L485).

### 2.2 `normalize_weights` of `LProjection`

This did not help in one run, which was killed. In `LProjection` ([`l_projection.py`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/l_projection.py#L298-L330)) each weight column, one per output feature, is rescaled without gradient to unit L2 norm over tables and rows at the start of every training forward pass. The `MultiHeadLut` docstring says after each backward; the code of `LProjection` does the first. Switched on for the attention tables of the byte-level model it ran far behind its twin and was stopped, because it fixes the output scale per table. It was not tried on nanochat. [exp099](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp099_nap6_normalize_weights), [early series notes](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/transformer_exps/SUMMARY.md#exp099-normalize_weights--stopped-early).
