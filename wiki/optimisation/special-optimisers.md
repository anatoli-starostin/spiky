# Special LUT optimisers

Short notes on optimisers designed for the LUT structure: one open idea, the optimisers that exist in the code, and the row-visit-aware and discrete-weight variants that did not beat plain Adam or Lion. This is a research direction, not an established topic: no dedicated LUT optimiser has been shown to beat the standard ones of [Basic optimisers](basic-optimisers.md). Each technique is one short subsection with a few sentences and links. No evaluation numbers are given here (the evidence is summarised in short paragraphs that link to the [Architectures](../architectures/README.md) pages). Most runs are single seeds at `e85a5ce`, with the old evaluation (see [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../CONVENTIONS.md)), and many were killed early.

## 1. Open idea

### 1.1 Walsh-basis, ranking-aligned table update

This is only an idea, untested, with no implementation found and a note on one ref as the only source. The input gradient of a lookup reveals which address bit the loss is reaching to flip next, but the usual table update throws that direction away. Take the margin $z_j$ of probe $j$ and its gradient $\partial L/\partial z_j$ from the input gradient. The step at which probe $j$ would flip, when positive, is $h_j=z_j/(\partial L/\partial z_j)$ and the probe with the smallest such step is called $j^\ast$ and marks the next bit the loss wants to flip. The proposal adds to the table update a dense step along one Walsh character,

$$\delta W[\ell]=-\eta g \chi_{j^\ast}(\ell)$$

where $g$ is the gradient arriving at the output, $\eta$ is the learning rate and $\chi_{j^\ast}(\ell)$ is the sign (plus or minus one) given by bit $j^\ast$ of the row address $\ell$ in the table. Reading: every row is pushed up or down according to one address bit, so rows on the two sides of the next flip move in opposite directions. In the Walsh basis a table reads $W[\ell]=\sum_S w_S\chi_S(\ell)$ and a hard row update shifts all coefficients $w_S$ alike, the soft backward favours low orders, meaning sets $S$ with few bits, and this update changes only the single coefficient of the one-bit set $S=\{j^\ast\}$ and nothing else.

*Against the Hamming ball gradient.* The ball backward of [Gradient backpropagation 3.2](../lookup-table-primitives/backpropagation.md#32-restricted-surrogates) writes only into the chosen row and its $n_\text{ap}$ one-bit neighbours, each weighted by its softmax mass. The Walsh update touches every row with weight plus or minus one, whatever its distance from the chosen row, and the sign depends only on bit $j^\ast$ of the address, and the note proposes it as an addition to the soft weights, not a replacement.

*Why no full table is needed.* The update is an outer product of two sign and value vectors: $\chi_{j^\ast}$ over the rows and $g$ over the output features. It can be applied as a rank-one correction, with each row reading its own address bit, without building a table-sized gradient. This is the note's remark, not something that was implemented.

The note reads the update as an anticipatory gradient, as a natural gradient with a metric that penalises high Walsh orders, or as a control variate. It proposes a probe on a small attention-input table of [exp428](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp428_qkv_tph_x2) against the soft backward, and two sanity checks (it should reduce to the soft backward when no probe is about to flip, and be checked on a two-pair toy table); none is recorded. The nearest things that were tried are the dominance gate (3.5) and the soft weight gradient, both dead or neutral. [`lut_gradient_notes.tex`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/lut_gradient_notes.tex#L131-L230).

## 2. Optimisers that exist in the code

### 2.1 [`BitPermutationLUTOptimizer`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/bit_permutation_lut_optimizer.py#L151-L591) and [`DraftBPLUTO`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/draft_bpluto.py#L137-L303)

We used this in production in the early bit-LUT runs, where it works, but the draft twin and a per-table latent scale did not help. Binary weights are the signs of a continuous latent. The optimiser keeps the latent and the Adam moments in low precision, projects the output gradient through the saved indices to a per-entry gradient, multiplies it by a straight-through gate on the latent, does a fused Adam step and packs the bits again. Plain Adam cannot be used because its second moment depends on the continuous latent while the effective parameter is a step function. A pure PyTorch twin (`DraftBPLUTO`) pins down the math, and a variant with a latent scale per table failed. The gate formula comes from a summary note; the article only calls it a minimal Adam variant. [bit_permutation_lut_optimizer.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/bit_permutation_lut_optimizer.py), [draft_bpluto.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/draft_bpluto.py), [article optimiser section](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/article_v2.tex#L405-L450), [bit_sweep_01](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/bit_sweep_01/sweep_results.csv), [exp305](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp305_draft_bpluto_vperpair).

### 2.2 [`MultiBitPermutationLUTOptimizer`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/multi_bit_permutation_lut_optimizer.py#L84-L221) and K-bit latents

It helped in one hybrid and did not help elsewhere. The same idea with a bf16 latent for weights of K bits, with a midrise quantiser (no level at zero) to avoid a dead zone at the start; K bits needed a longer warmup. The hybrid with a K-bit output projection and one-bit queries, keys and values was the best table-efficient setup at context 128, while making the queries and keys K-bit too hurt and the early nanochat runs with K equal to 4 trailed K equal to 1. It is not known whether it is still used after the move to the Tiny family of modules. [multi_bit_permutation_lut_optimizer.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/multi_bit_permutation_lut_optimizer.py), [exp347](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp347_cfc_out_multibit_k4), [permutational direction note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_permutational_direction.md).

### 2.3 [`TinyMultiHeadLutOptimizer`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/tiny_multi_head_lut_optimizer.py#L83-L226), bf16 state with stochastic rounding

We tried this in the [exp061](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp061_tiny_mhlut) variants and did not use it later, so whether it helps is unknown. It is AdamW for the real-valued tables with bf16 moments and unbiased stochastic rounding of the weight updates, so that steps below the bf16 resolution survive. Early variants ended behind fp32 (two had bugs); the last fixed variants were within noise of fp32 at 2400 and 4200 steps but were killed, and no run completed after the fix. The bug story is read from run headers. Later runs use an in-script AdamW or Lion with bf16 storage and an fp32 master copy (see [Training efficiency 4.1](training-efficiency.md#41-bf16-table-storage-with-an-fp32-master-copy)). [tiny_multi_head_lut_optimizer.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/tiny_multi_head_lut_optimizer.py), [exp061](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp061_tiny_mhlut).

## 3. Other things we tried

Each paragraph says what was done and what happened. None of these beat plain Adam or Lion; most are single short runs, and all that were killed are said so.

### 3.1 Sparse-row-aware Adam variants and per-row learning rates

None of these helped, and the runs were killed early; the optimiser classes live in the experiment folders, not in the library. Several optimisers tracked how often each row of each table was visited: moments updated only on rows touched in the step, no decay on untouched rows (the minimal variant tracked plain Adam), a step amplified by the visit count (it blew up), count-weighted moments, damping of cold rows, and a learning-rate scale per row from an average of visits (cold rows up to twice the step). None recovered any of the gap between a small and a large batch. The notes list sparse-aware Adam as planned at one date and as failed at another, and there is no dedicated note for [exp375](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp375_sparse_row_adamw) to [exp381](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp381_sparse_cold_damp_t8). [exp375](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp375_sparse_row_adamw), [exp376 `sparse_skip_adamw.py`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp376_sparse_skip_adamw/sparse_skip_adamw.py), [exp381](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp381_sparse_cold_damp_t8), [exp359](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp359_row_lr_visit_ema/train.py), [smooth against Tiny note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_mhlut_smooth_beats_tinymhlut.md).

### 3.2 Lazy Adam and the cell-hit census

Lazy Adam did not help, and this is a negative diagnostic. It advances the moments of a row only on the steps in which the row is hit. A diagnostic showed almost no staleness at the small table size used (64 rows per table, almost every row hit at almost every step), and the comparison was a tie. A census at a converged checkpoint found that rare buckets are empty, which removed the motivation for per-cell-hit rules at that size. At larger table sizes hits per row are far fewer, and the d24 gradient spectra report skewed hit counts, so this does not settle the large case. [exp_n_0073](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0073_sparse_adam_lut/README.md), [exp_n_0075](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0075_cell_hit_gap_diag/README.md).

### 3.3 Cell-level, direction-preserving momentum

This did not help, in tests at a short horizon only. Each cell kept a vector first moment and a scalar second moment, so that a cell moves as a whole along its averaged gradient (in the manner of LARS or LAMB). It gave no gain over element-wise Adam and was worse with a larger first beta. [exp_n_0076](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0076_cell_level_momentum/README.md).

### 3.4 Gauss-Newton style update from visit counts

This did not help; the run was killed and the exact final number is not in the notes. A table is linear in its weights, so the exact diagonal of the Hessian is the visit count of each row. A damped momentum divided by the visit count plus a damping constant landed between SGD and Adam or Lion, as a "denoised SGD". The visit-count stash stays in the library, off by default. [dead ends note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_convergence_levers_dead_ends.md), [visit-count stash](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/tiny_multi_head_lut.py#L109-L124), [exp455](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp455_gn_lut).

### 3.5 Dominance gate on the weight gradient

This did not help: the single run was much worse than the baseline from the first evaluation, so an implementation issue is not ruled out, and there is no note on it. The idea starts from the fact that the next LUT layer reads only the order of the output coordinates (which one is larger than which), not their values. So a coordinate of the selected row only matters through its position above or below the others, and a gradient that merely pushes an already correctly placed coordinate further away from the rest changes nothing downstream. The gate keeps the gradient of a coordinate only if the step would move it towards the row mean, that is, against its current position; the other coordinates get zero gradient. In addition, the mean over the coordinates is removed from the gradient, because shifting all coordinates together does not change their order either. The gate is a switch in the soft-mode backward of the tiny LUT layer, applied to the row that was read. [flags in tiny_multi_head_lut.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/tiny_multi_head_lut.py#L83-L110), [exp443](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp443_dominance_gate).

The same group of switches also holds a per-row mean normalisation of the weight gradient ([Training efficiency 8.5](training-efficiency.md#85-per-row-mean-normalisation-of-the-weight-gradient)), a soft weight gradient that was neutral at 89M ([exp445](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp445_soft_wgrad_hard_fwd), explained as gradient math on the backpropagation page) and a hard-sign backward; all were dead ends or neutral.

### 3.6 Gradient-free row revival

This is only an idea, built and smoke tested, and it may never have run. Every so often, rows with very low traffic are rewritten as a copy of the busiest row of the same table plus noise, and their moments are reset. At a trained checkpoint very few rows are starved, so a neutral outcome was expected. It is not clear whether the run was launched and lost, or never launched. [exp_g_0013](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_g_0013/README.md).

### 3.7 Discrete-weight optimisers

This did not help. A clean teacher-student benchmark for discrete weights compared fp32 and fp8 latents, the bit optimiser of 2.1, a ladder of four states and ladder SGD; the ladder results exist only in csv and png files and were not tabulated. On the language model, a bit-flip optimiser (steps of plus or minus one) stalled, and int8 quantisation-aware training with quantised gradients stuck far behind. [bitflip_clean](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/bitflip_clean), [exp281](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp281_bitflip), [exp147](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp147_int8_qat).
