# Training efficiency

Short notes on the techniques we tried to make training cheaper or faster: less wall clock, fewer inputs seen for the same loss (input efficiency in transformers), or fewer samples in general. Each technique is one short subsection: a few sentences and links. The evidence level varies a lot. Many runs are single seeds, some were killed early, some results are from a short proxy run, and some notes contradict each other; each subsection says so where it applies. No evaluation numbers are given here (the evidence is summarised in short paragraphs that link to the [Architectures](../architectures/README.md) pages). Most nanochat runs at `e85a5ce` use the old evaluation (small validation set, first-rows bias, see [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../CONVENTIONS.md)), so only differences between matched runs mean something there.

## 1. Batch and sample efficiency

### 1.1 Batch size is the biggest lever, and accumulation reproduces it

This works in our runs, but only from single seeds and the old evaluation. In every series the effective batch was the largest single improvement. Gradient accumulation of small micro-batches tracked the native large batch closely ([exp367](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp367_grad_accum_8) against [exp363](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp363_bs128)), so the gap to the large batch is gradient quality, not a matmul-shape effect. Accumulation costs proportionally more wall clock per step, so it is a way to emulate a large batch, not a speed-up. [accumulation note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_grad_accum_reproduces_big_batch.md), [exp367](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp367_grad_accum_8), [exp608 note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_exp608_bs96_lut_beats_vanilla.md).

### 1.2 Is the batch effect specific to LUTs?

The sources conflict on this. Early notes explained the batch benefit by sparse per-row gradients. A direct test with the vanilla model at the same batches found that vanilla gained at least as much at small scale; later notes at about 289M parameters and the [exp428](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp428_qkv_tph_x2) report say the LUT model gains more. The memory-bank index still carries the earlier hypothesis. A claim needs a vanilla comparison at matched batch and steps (vanilla at batch 96 for [exp608](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp608_v_lut_tph512_bs96): [exp609](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp609_vanilla_rope_bs96_8k), 1.1812 against 1.2180). [session notes 2026-05-12](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/SESSION_2026_05_12_NOTES.md#exp270271--vanilla-gpt-2-batch-sweep--disproves-lut-specific-batch-story), [exp428 report](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/exp428_report.tex#L381-L384).

### 1.3 Gradient density as an explanation

The measurement is real, but the cause is only a reading and was not tested. A vanilla weight sees gradient from every input of a batch, a table row only from the inputs routed to it, so the table gradient is noisier. Comparing [exp486](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp486_bs48_8k) with a small-batch run suggested two regimes, quantity limited early and precision limited late. A cell-hit count on the hyperplane line found almost every cell hit on almost every step at small table size, which argues against a row-starvation story there. The proposed direct test (smaller tables, more inputs per row) was never run. [convergence note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_convergence_bottleneck.md), [exp_n_0075](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0075_cell_hit_gap_diag/README.md).

### 1.4 More inputs per run, or a different batch shape

More inputs help here, while the effect of batch shape alone is mixed. On the hyperplane line the gap to dense closed when the batch grew 1.5 times, but a control with the same total inputs recovered most of that gain, so mostly the total number of inputs mattered. An asymmetric accumulation test pointed to a small shape effect at most. Old evaluation, and the larger batch also changed the validation size. [exp_n_0046](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0046_H8_d48_tph64_bs1p5x_16k/README.md), [exp_n_0078](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0078_asym_lut_gradaccum/README.md).

### 1.5 Early gains fade with a longer horizon

We saw this repeatedly, with one seed per arm. Effects seen at 16k steps shrank when rerun at 48k, and every intervention tried on the d24 LUT feed-forward (table whitening, projection Muon, reading two rows, a larger table, a higher learning rate) gave an early advantage that faded. The survey author reads this as the function class binding; that reading is not tested. A short proxy can therefore rank techniques wrongly. [LIGHTMHL survey](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs_corrected/LIGHTMHL_SURVEY.md#the-same-knobs-at-48k--every-one-of-them-shrinks), [forks analysis](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/analysis/2026-10-16_forks/README.md).

### 1.6 Cheap proxies and measurement traps

This is methodology that works for us. A 4k step proxy sweep with its own dense reference is cheap, with a measured noise of sd 0.0096; seed replicas give a noise estimate that is only a lower bound. Short runs must keep the full length schedule and be truncated, because a short schedule cools the learning rate and flatters the run. Two bugs to remember: the data loader reuses one buffer, so "independent micro-batches" can alias the same data (clone each batch), and interleaving forward passes with the loader's asynchronous copy corrupted later batches. [SWEEP_README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs_corrected/SWEEP_README.md), [FIXED_EVAL](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/FIXED_EVAL.md#reused-buffer-safety).

## 2. Learning rates and schedules

### 2.1 Schedule and warmup

We use this recipe, and the variations we tried were neutral or worse. Two families are used: warmup then cosine to a floor (LUT language-model and ffn_replacement runs) and warmup, constant, long linear warmdown (nanochat, lut_nanochat). A higher cosine floor, warm restarts and a flat rate did not help. A fresh warmup is needed after a merge or hand-off, and a resume with a fresh schedule was clearly worse than one longer clean run. No source compares the two families for LUT models. [nanochat EXPERIMENTS](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/EXPERIMENTS.md), [exp440](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp440_cosine_floor_05), [exp759](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp759_exp756_stdsched_8k).

### 2.2 A separate learning rate for the tables

This helped slightly in small single-seed runs and was flat elsewhere. On the tiny soft-LUT architecture a table learning rate about three times the global one beat the global rate by a small margin with a shallow optimum. In the early byte-level series the unembedder wanted a much higher rate than the inner tables. On the hyperplane line a higher cosine floor for the tables changed nothing. The best table rate may depend on batch and was not re-swept. [lr sweep note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_lr_sweep_tiny.md), [exp008](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp008_lut_unembedder_lr_split), [exp_n_0082](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0082_H6_d64_tph36_bs1p5x_lutfloor50_16k/README.md).

### 2.3 Square-root learning-rate rescale with the batch

We do not know whether this helps. nanochat scales all rates by the square root of the batch ratio; the LUT runs switch it off. One run with it on (its folder is mislabelled "nolrrescale" and it was aborted) was ahead early and behind later, the same pattern as in 1.5. Nothing isolates the effect, and it is a single seed. [run config](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/results/d24-lutffn-confn1-1xh100-s0-full-ABORTED-nolrrescale/run_config.json).

### 2.4 Learning rate and schedule for the tables: never tuned

This is only an open question, not tested. Every LUT run of the ffn_replacement line reuses the dense recipe unchanged. The survey lists a learning rate and schedule control for the table population as the one untried single-run intervention that fits the 48k evidence. [LIGHTMHL survey, section II.6](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs_corrected/LIGHTMHL_SURVEY.md#L1314-L1324).

### 2.5 Global gradient-norm clipping

This helped slightly, but only with bf16 table storage, and the sources conflict for dense models. Clipping recovered part of the drift of a bf16 head and gave a small gain in the [`FastMultiHeadLut`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/fast_multi_head_lut.py#L749-L943) line, below the stated noise and not isolated at 16k. The shipped `lutgpt` recipe says clipping is required for bf16 storage and optional for fp32 tables, while the upstream dense harness removed it and the d24 LUT runs use none. [exp750](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp750_FastMHL_exp737v2_clip_all_4K), [lutgpt README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/examples/lutgpt/README.md#L76-L78).

## 3. Initialisation

### 3.1 Tables and residual outputs

This works in our runs, with a small uniform table init and a zero decompress weight; larger or all-zero inits did not help. The default is a small uniform or Gaussian table init with the decompress weight at zero, so the feed-forward starts silent. Larger table inits were clearly worse, zero tables stalled early (no gradient reaches them; that run, [exp366](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp366_zero_init_out_resid), was killed early, so part of the stall may be warm-up), and additive residual branches must start at zero. A tied dot-product head needs a small norm gain at init or the loss starts at chance level. [lut_ffn.py init](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/nanochat/nanochat/lut_ffn.py#L1-L26), [exp366](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp366_zero_init_out_resid), [exp757](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp757_exp750_zero_init_residuals_4K).

## 4. Precision and memory

### 4.1 bf16 table storage with an fp32 master copy

We use this in the `FastMultiHeadLut` recipe, from a single seed at 4k steps. Storing the tables in bf16 with an fp32 master copy and fp32 momentum inside Lion cut memory traffic and step time and gave a small quality gain, traced to bf16 gradient accumulation noise rather than to bf16 compute. Without the master copy it drifted. In the older multi-alternative module bf16 weights were slower and larger. The journey note lists bf16 weights as a dead end while [`examples/lutgpt`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/examples/lutgpt) ships them (conflict, probably speed against quality or different code versions); whether the gain survives 16k is only partly answered. [exp737](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp737_FastMHL_bf16_weights_4K), [summary 2026-05-28 to 06-11](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/summary_2026-05-28_to_2026-06-11.md#2026-06-10--exp733-749-bf16-lut-storage-bf16-head-exploration), [lutgpt train.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/examples/lutgpt/train.py#L316-L386).

### 4.2 bf16 head or attention in training; bf16 at inference

Training the head or attention in bf16 did not help, while bf16 at inference works. Running the unembedder or the attention in bf16 during training drifted behind the fp32 run in every variant tried. The diagnostic scripts attribute this to Lion's sign step amplifying small noise on sparse rows; that is a diagnosis, not an ablation. Casting all weights to bf16 after training changed validation by less than the evaluation noise. [diag_drift_mechanism.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/diag_drift_mechanism.py), [eval_exp750_bf16_head.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/eval_exp750_bf16_head.py).

### 4.3 fp8 against bf16 and fp32 latents for bit LUTs

A bf16 latent works, and an fp8 latent state did not help. With the soft backward fixed, a bf16 latent matched fp32 while an fp8 latent and moments cost a large amount of loss, so the fp8 state, not the one-bit forward, was the problem. A forward-only fp8 rounding test showed no large cost. The expectation that fp8 noise might act as a regulariser is recorded as unverified. The fp8 study script stores no result files. A sine penalty that pulls weights to a quantisation grid is on [Regularisation 3.2](regularisation.md#32-a-sine-penalty-pulling-weights-to-a-quantisation-grid). [exp312](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp312_bf16_soft), [exp310](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp310_fp8_soft).

### 4.4 bf16 and fp8 projections, and the fp8 backbone

Speed was measured but quality was never isolated, so we do not know the net effect. bf16 compress and decompress projections saved a small share of step time and memory on the H100. The block profile showed fp8 projections no better than bf16 at these sizes. The d24 backbone is fp8 in all runs, and upstream measurements call that a modest net speed-up. The profile comes partly from a rescued shell log and a re-check on the H100 was pending, so these timings are provisional. [RTX 5090 profile](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/profiling/results/gpustar-rtx5090-2026-10-09/FINDINGS.md#speed-hw-dep), [upstream LOG, fp8](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/nanochat/dev/LOG.md#L317-L384).

### 4.5 Optimiser state and memory

This comes from accounting, not from an experiment. In the d24 LUT feed-forward run the tables dominate the optimiser state (fp32 AdamW moments over hundreds of millions of table entries); Lion would halve it, and projections on Muon save peak memory. Distributed runs impose divisibility constraints on table dimensions, and the table whitening is single rank only. Compressing the value embeddings is described and not implemented. [nanochat state doc](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/docs/nanochat-state-2026-10.md), [lut_nanochat README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/README.md#stage-2a-compressing-the-value-embeddings-not-yet-implemented).

## 5. Wall clock

### 5.1 What saves time

A step-time benchmark at [exp760](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp760_exp756_24k) scale found that fewer tables per head saves time linearly in the table count, and the hard forward (one gather instead of a blend of rows) saves a large share of hybrid time. Shrinking the stream width saves little because the residual tables and the unembedder dominate; the earlier belief that width was the big win was wrong. Reinvesting saved time into a larger batch at the same step count lost little quality at far fewer parameters (one run). The benchmark matched a real run to a few percent for one case only. [wall leverboard note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_lut_wall_leverboard.md), [exp764 note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_exp764_tph_halved_tiny_pareto.md), [bench_exp760_vs_E96.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/bench_exp760_vs_E96.py).

### 5.2 Training-path speed-ups

Measured speed-ups, with no change in convergence reported and several items from rescued logs: bf16 backward and a tensor-core forward for the plus or minus one attention, a compiled soft module far faster than the first soft implementation, a weight-gradient path through a sparse product instead of atomic scatter, the confidence-weighted read as one embedding bag, int32 cell indices (gradients bit identical), fused CUDA cartridges with whole-model compile, and chunked backward to avoid a size fault. [session 2026-05-05](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/nanochat_exps/session_2026-05-05.md), [lutorch_ex README, compile and fused](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/3ac72cca40c63b77d509d4ccff33bb85d43e515e/src/spiky/lutorch_ex/README.md#L232-L252).

### 5.3 Determinism switch

`set_deterministic(True)` with deterministic algorithms gave bitwise identical checkpoints for the BitPermutationLUT line at about 6.5 percent per-step slowdown (sort based scatter instead of atomics). Useful for noise floors; the notes do not say it was used in the nanochat runs. [transformer experiments summary note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_transformer_exp_summary.md).

## 6. Soft to hard and temperatures

How the gradient behaves between a soft and a hard forward is on [Gradient backpropagation](../lookup-table-primitives/backpropagation.md#13-value-and-gradient-decoupling) and [2.4](../lookup-table-primitives/backpropagation.md#24-the-blend-as-value-and-gradient); only the training-process side is here. That page has no section on the soft-to-hard gap itself, so the nearest sections are [2](../lookup-table-primitives/backpropagation.md#2-neighbour-surrogates) and [3](../lookup-table-primitives/backpropagation.md#3-softmax-surrogates-over-rows) (the neighbour and the softmax surrogates).

### 6.1 Soft-to-hard gap and native hard training

Native hard training is our recipe, though the gap is not fully closed. A model trained with a soft or blended forward and evaluated with the hard single-row forward loses a clear amount, whatever the backward. Training natively in hard mode with a dense all-rows backward closed most of it, and the saved wall clock can go into more hard steps. Post-hoc hardening of a smooth model was much worse. Old evaluation for the `e85a5ce` runs. [LUTGPT report](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/lutgpt_research_report.tex#L776-L850), [exp731 note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_exp731_fastmhl_hard_densek_sota.md), [hard-forward directive](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_hard_forward_is_the_goal.md).

### 6.2 Soft-to-hard fine-tune and hand-off

This works with conditions at small scale and is mixed at large scale, and several runs were killed. Switching from a soft backward to the hard forward and fine-tuning a few thousand steps beat the soft model at small scale and beat hard-from-scratch at matched compute. At the larger batch it did not reproduce: the fine-tune stayed behind, a fresh warmup destabilised the soft weights, no warmup was worse, and half the peak rate without warmup helped a little. Native hard training (6.1) is the cleaner route. [exp713](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp713_soft_to_hard_finetune), [exp715](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp715_exp710_soft_to_hard), [exp717](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp717_half_peak_no_warmup).

### 6.3 Learnable temperatures against fixed ones

This helped marginally. Per-module learnable temperatures (log-parametrised) helped slightly over fixed ones, and freezing the blend temperature at the measured margin was clearly worse than learning it; learned values settle several times above the margin scale. For the hard-forward read the two backward temperatures changed little. The difference between two close hyperplane runs is within noise. [exp248](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp248_soft_lut_learnable), [exp_n_0033](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0033_H16_d24_tph64_learntemp_16k/README.md), [LIGHTMHL survey](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/runs_corrected/LIGHTMHL_SURVEY.md).

## 7. Distillation and auxiliary ideas

### 7.1 Logit distillation harness

Distillation works for a dense student, while for a LUT student it is only an idea. [`distill.py`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/nanochat/nanochat/distill.py) implements a temperature-scaled KL against a frozen teacher (in log space for the large vocabulary) with an optional blend of hard cross entropy. A fresh dense student distilled from a trained dense teacher is far more sample efficient than ordinary training. The README makes this a go or no-go check every LUT variant must pass, but no LUT student has been trained with it; the reference curve is provisional and the alpha 0.5 run crashed near step 1950. [distill.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/nanochat/nanochat/distill.py), [lut_nanochat README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/README.md#stage-2-entry-gate-the-distillation-screen-every-lut-variant-must-pass).

### 7.2 Distillation as a design-search tool

This is useful as a search tool, but the transfer to end-to-end training failed in one case. Small LUT candidates are fitted offline to input and output pairs taken from a trained layer or head and ranked by sign accuracy or KL; the best shape then goes to end-to-end training. The per-layer probe found the first layer nearly linear and the middle layers hardest, which motivated more tables on early layers and helped end to end (the gain mixes reallocation with more tables); the winning small-table shape itself regressed end to end. [distill_exp338](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/distill_exp338), [exp354](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp354_graded_out_tph_upper), [ffn_replacement distill README](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/c4a750b2f9ab65efe6a61254103a92d6fd8fc2a2/experiments/ffn_replacement/distill/README.md#results-runssweep_0238arch_16k-exp_n_0238-architecture-16000-steps).

### 7.3 A dense head as a convergence aid

This is unresolved. In the CIFAR-era LUT networks a dense MLP head on the same LUT features converged several times faster than the LUT classifier, because a table entry only gets gradient from the few batches that select it; more epochs were the main fix. The article lists training a differentiable HyperLUT and converting it to tables as future work; the conversion was never done. [CIFAR journal](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/experiments/experiments.md#L516-L545).

### 7.4 Open ideas

Nothing here was run to a result. 

- **Auxiliary detached "backward LUT" loss** ([exp711](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp711_backward_lut)): a loss per block that predicts the block input from its output through a mirror LUT, to densify supervision. The run has empty metrics. [exp711](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp711_backward_lut).
- **Tricks of tiny recursive models** for a LUT recursion: deep supervision with detached carried state, weight EMA, gradients through the last recursion pass only, adam-atan2. Only launch configs of a dense fast baseline exist, no LUT run. [trm.md](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/2175d6904f1fd41f17b1ee8049ad685fa26a43ea/doc/research/trm.md).

## 8. Other things we tried

Each paragraph says what was done and what happened. None of these helped; most are single runs, and several were killed early.

### 8.1 Hard-example mining at fixed batch

We tried this once, the run was killed at step 1800, and it did not help. A larger batch was run forward without gradient and only the hardest sequences were back-propagated. It was consistently worse than a plain small batch and far from the true large batch, at several times the forward cost. The reading, not tested, is that biased selection distorts the gradient while the large batch helps through unbiased averaging. [note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_hard_mining_tiny_lut.md), [exp358](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp358_hard_mine_48_16).

### 8.2 Inverse-frequency loss weighting and a late-layer learning-rate multiplier

We tried this twice, both runs were killed early, and it did not help. The large-batch gain was found to sit in the last layers and on rare vocabulary, so two fixed-batch imitations were tried: weighting the loss by inverse frequency (zero-sum inside a batch) and raising the learning rate of the last layers' tables. Both were worse than the plain run. The reading is that the deficit is noise, not under-movement. [bs48 note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_bs48_what_improves.md).

### 8.3 Densifying table gradients with data tricks

Two attempts against rare-cluster starvation did not help: generating table rows from a small shared network with a smoothness prior (a large regression, the rows need their own capacity) and a mixup shadow batch at embedding level (slightly worse). Coverage was already nearly complete, so there was little to densify. [hypernet exp_n_0049](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0049_H8_d48_tph64_hypernet/README.md), [mixup exp_n_0072](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0072_mixup_shadow_batch/README.md).

### 8.4 Batch ramps and tiny batches

Starting with a tiny batch and growing it did not beat a fixed batch, and the comparisons are not at equal data. The idea was to combine the token efficiency of small batches early in training with the lower gradient noise of large batches late (see 1.1 for why the batch matters). In the early bit-LUT series on bytes, a run started at batch 1 and doubled the batch at fixed steps up to 32, using about as many tokens as the fixed run with batch 8; it ended worse than that fixed batch-8 run. A fixed batch of 1 plateaued far behind it, although it saw many times fewer tokens, so this does not show that small batches are bad per token. In the later byte-level series a ramp from 16 to 128 over 100k steps was tried; its comparison runs differ in length and data, so it is not a clean test. We keep a fixed batch. [exp038](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp038_bs_ramp), [exp018](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp018_dqk32_heads6), [exp037](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp037_dqk32_heads6_bs1), [exp144](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp144_batch_schedule_100k).

### 8.5 Per-row mean normalisation of the weight gradient

We tried this in a single run with the old evaluation and it did not help. In the soft backward of `TinyMultiHeadLut` each table row's weight gradient is divided by the number of batch elements that landed on that row, so the sum over visiting inputs becomes a mean and cold rows are not starved. The switch is `_GLOBAL_PER_ROW_GRAD_NORM` (set with `set_per_row_grad_norm`, off by default; code at `e85a5ce`), and [exp441](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp441_per_row_grad_norm) was worse than the reference at every checked step. No note explains why; a plausible reason, not tested, is that Adam or Lion already normalise per coordinate. [flag in tiny_multi_head_lut.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/tiny_multi_head_lut.py#L72-L81), [exp441](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp441_per_row_grad_norm).

### 8.6 Speed attempts that failed

These attempts did not help. Skipping dropped-table entries through a padding index made the read slower. A custom Triton weight-gradient kernel could not beat the compiler's fusion. Stepping AdamW every other iteration was worse in wall clock upstream. fp8 attention through a scaled matmul was rejected for batching and error reasons, and the probabilistic forward cannot be compiled and is far slower. [RTX 5090 profile](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/b38b57d70f4c30f877888ac148f9d9e60495b00d/experiments/lut_nanochat/profiling/results/gpustar-rtx5090-2026-10-09/FINDINGS.md#ranked-candidate-optimisations), [wgrad ceiling note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_hybrid_smooth_wgrad_compile_ceiling.md).

### 8.7 Soft-forward annealing with adaptive hand-off

We tried one schedule and one threshold with the old evaluation and it did not help. [`SoftAnnealLut`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0054_H8_d48_tph64_softanneal_16k/soft_anneal_lut.py#L46-L102) runs a softmax-weighted forward over all rows with both temperatures decayed exponentially and hands off to the hard forward when the mean top-1 softmax mass crosses a threshold. The soft phase optimised a blended objective, the hard-evaluated metric stayed far behind at hand-off, and the final model ended behind the hard-forward control. [exp_n_0054](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0054_H8_d48_tph64_softanneal_16k/README.md).

### 8.8 Forced temperature annealing

This failed repeatedly, with several runs killed before 4k steps. Annealing the softmax or selection temperature toward argmax failed repeatedly: soft mixtures annealed over several ranges regressed or stalled, a start that is too soft cannot discriminate rows, and attention temperature anneals in the early series and a smooth-to-hard switch at 50k were worse than a constant temperature. A trainable-anchor variant with an annealed temperature lost to random anchors. [early series notes](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/transformer_exps/experiments.md#L507-L516), [exp345](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp345_soft_anneal_05_001), [exp483](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp483_ct_nap9_anneal_tsel).

### 8.9 Temperature penalty to make a soft model hardenable

This did not help, and the hardening numbers come from a config description. An extra loss term on the learnable selection temperature pushed it down and left the soft quality unchanged, yet hardening the checkpoint to argmax still lost a clear amount, and a tenfold stronger penalty did not give a hardenable solution. [matmul softmax note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_matmul_mhlut_softmax.md), [exp494](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp494_softmax_temppenalty), [exp495](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp495_temppenalty_strong).

### 8.10 Curriculum from small tables, merged by an additive rule

This did not help, and was at best neutral in one narrow recipe. Training starts with small tables and merges sibling tables into a larger parent by an outer-add rule that keeps the function unchanged at the merge. The mechanism is smooth, but multi-stage chains ended far behind (bloated final architecture, a hiccup at each merge, low late learning rate); a two-stage version tied the baseline within seed noise. Lessons: end at a moderate table size, fresh warmup per stage, constant batch. [curriculum note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_curriculum_nap_merge.md), [exp415 note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_exp415_curriculum_nap3_to_6_negative.md).

### 8.11 Progressive growth of the table count

This is only an idea, unfinished with no final result found. Two schemes were started: a wrapper that adds bit-LUTs at fixed steps, and a loop that grows each module in proportion to its output-gradient norm. The signal was extremely skewed toward layer 0 and misleading for the query and key projections. [exp059](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp059_growing_luts), [grow loop NOTES](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/notes/spiky/transformer_exps/exp374_grow_loop/NOTES.md).

### 8.12 Distillation from a vanilla teacher into the LUT model

This did not help, though the sources partly conflict. Four attempts to distil a trained vanilla transformer into the LUT language model (a frozen transplanted head, a layer-aligned representation loss, large-batch student-only runs) did not catch up with plain training. The paper's diagnosis is that teacher logits are too low-temperature for discrete routing to follow; this is a diagnosis, not tested. Freezing a converged unembedder gave a large early lead and ended worse, and per-block distillation of a dense feed-forward into a LUT block followed by a swap was much worse than end-to-end training. The memory index still lists distillation as the one untried lever, and the journey note says it "moves the needle"; both conflict with these results. [exp428 report](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/secondary/papers/Permutations-is-all-you-need/paper/exp428_report.tex#L329-L334), [exp459](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp459_frozen_unembedder), [exp_n_0055](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/experiments/hyperplane_ffn/exp_n_0055_distill_dense_to_lut_16k/README.md).
