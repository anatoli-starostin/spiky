# Unembedders

The output layer of a language model maps the hidden width to the vocabulary: a $D \times V$ matrix, 12.6M weights at width 384 and vocabulary 32768, read in full for every token. It is one of the two matrix multiplications that remain in a LUT model with LUT SDPA (the other is the attention score, see [LUT attention](lut-attention.md#lut-sdpa)). The June 2026 report names a LUT-based unembedder as an open problem: the difficulty is producing magnitude-coded logits over 32768 classes from a rank-coded representation, in a way that keeps the gradient signal a cross-entropy loss expects. This page collects what we tried: LUT heads of several kinds, code scoring, a vector-quantised vocabulary, weight tying, and why the plain linear head stayed.

**Scales.** The early heads were tried at byte level (vocabulary 257, context 32 or 128), where a direct table over the vocabulary is cheap, and at the NanoChat BPE scale (vocabulary 32768, context 512, the fast test scale of [Transformers](README.md)), where it is not. Numbers are old-evaluation numbers of [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../../CONVENTIONS.md), single seeds, comparable only inside their own batch of runs; byte-level numbers are validation cross-entropy, BPE numbers bits per byte.

## Idea / model

A dense head computes logits $l_v = h \cdot u_v$ for the hidden vector $h$ and one row $u_v$ per vocabulary entry $v$; a tied head uses the input embedding of $v$ as $u_v$. Any LUT replacement must produce 32768 numbers from a few table reads. The families we tried:

- a **LUT over the vocabulary**: a multi-head LUT whose output width is the vocabulary (sparse scatter: each table adds its row to a few logits), at byte level also with multi-bit votes;
- **code scoring**: give each token a code of a few symbols, and let each head score the symbols; the logit of a token is the sum of the scores of its symbols (the vector-quantised vocabulary [`VocabLUT`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/vq_vocab_lut.py), the scalar-gather [`ScoreLUT`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/score_lut_head.py), and the hyperplane code-scoring head [`HyperplaneCodeLUT`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/0c35e3be8deab30338681013158ecbd166548764/src/spiky/lutorch/hyperplane_code_lut.py#L55-L159));
- **tying** the head to the input embedding, with or without a LUT.

## What was found

**A dense linear head stayed.** It was reached from two sides.

- At byte level, replacing the table unembedder by a linear layer gave 1.6195 against 1.6302 with fewer parameters ([`exp100`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp100_nap6_linear_unemb), [`exp097`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp097_no_ffn_tph512_nap6)); the table head needed three alternatives per read to work at all (1.6403 with one alternative against 1.5031 with three, [`exp005`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp005_lut_unembedder), [`exp006`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp006_lut_unembedder_nalt3)) and its outputs were unstable (mean $-37$, standard deviation 27.6) ([SUMMARY](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/SUMMARY.md#phase-9-linear-unembedder--nap-ablation-exp099102), phase 9). A head made only of bit tables and a multi-bit logit table reached 1.3721 against 1.379 for a variant with a dominance table followed by a linear layer ([`exp415`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp415_multibit_logits), [`exp409`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/transformer_exps/exp409_final_lut_unembed_short), note) in a short byte-level run ([permutational page](permutational.md#what-was-found); vocabulary 257 only).
- A wide MLP head (a hidden layer of 1024) on a fixed-width backbone at BPE scale gave no gain over the linear head: 1.4891 against 1.4825, 0.0066 worse, one seed each ([`exp566`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp566_big_head_mlp), [`exp513`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp513_qk_argmax_nap4_tph256)); the gap is probably within seed noise, so this is one test without a gain, not a failure. The [note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_big_head_mlp_falsified.md) reads it as an early lead that the schedule washed out, and the gain of widening the residual stream in an earlier sweep as coming from the width of the stream, not from the head; that is the note's reading, not a tested ablation. This is a dense head on a LUT backbone, not a LUT head.
- Every model of the LUT SDPA and FFN lines uses `Linear(D, V)`, untied.

**Fair comparison.** In a dense model, tying the head to the embedding is a strong regulariser for small models: untying the dense baseline cost 0.0261 bits per byte (1.3882 tied, 1.4143 untied at 8k steps, 23.2M and 35.8M parameters, [`exp328`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp328_minimal_gpt_rope_bs16), [`exp476`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp476_untied_emb_head)). A LUT model whose head reads the Euclidean stream cannot share weights with an embedding of another width, so the fair comparison for an untied LUT head is the untied dense model; this changed the apparent gap of the LUT models by about a quarter ([note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_fair_vanilla_baseline_untied.md), [exp476](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp476_untied_emb_head), [exp328](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp328_minimal_gpt_rope_bs16)).

### Code scoring and the vector-quantised vocabulary

On 2026-05-25 a set of matmul-free head designs was tried against a trained backbone with a fixed width of 384 and a linear-head reference of 1.4825 at 8k steps ([note](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/claude-memory/main-h100/memory-bank/project_unembedder_head_explorations.md), claimed; no clean summary files):

- the vector-quantised [`VocabLUT`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/vq_vocab_lut.py) (a per-token code of sub-codebook entries, logit as the dot product of the hidden vector with the concatenated entries) was the best matmul-free head, near the linear head at 8 to 26 times compression; offline product quantisation of a trained linear head needs a sub-vector size of 2 to be near-lossless;
- [`ScoreLUT`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/src/spiky/lutorch/score_lut_head.py) (a scalar gather per code symbol) was about 0.2 behind, after a symmetry trap in its initialisation was fixed;
- other rank-based scoring heads gave no gain; the note's key finding is that a plain tied dot product with a narrow rank stream (width 64) beat every rank-based readout, and that the 0.13 to 0.15 gap to the linear head came from the narrowing of the residual stream (width 64 against 384), not from the design of the head (1.6283 tied and 1.6171 untied against about 1.48 for the wide stream, [`exp560`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp560_tied_dot_E64), [`exp561`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp561_untied_E64); [exp560](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp560_tied_dot_E64), [exp561](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp561_untied_E64)).

This is the reason the dual stream has a wide Euclidean stream (see [Permutational transformers](permutational.md#the-dual-stream)): the classifier wants magnitudes and a high dimension.

## Open ideas

- Vector quantisation of the head offers the best compression that stays near the linear head (8 to 26 times, claimed); it has not been trained end to end in the final models, and it is where a LUT unembedder would start.
- *Weight tying of a LUT head.* In the dense baseline tying helps (see above). For a small matmul-free LUT head tied to the input embedding we made only a few attempts, and the analysis of them is very vague. The attempts: a test that the embedding of every token decodes back to itself through the head ([tied_lut_inverse_test.py](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/blob/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/workbooks/tied_lut_inverse_test.py), [`exp514`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp514_tied_lut_unembedder) to [`exp522`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp522_singlestream_aux025)), and three language-model runs that were stopped early at steps 2,800 to 3,400 of 8,000: [`exp518`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp518_tied_sparse_k4), [`exp521`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp521_sparse_lut_noaux) and [`exp522`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp522_singlestream_aux025), at 2.12, 2.11 and 2.29 bits per byte against 1.63 for the linear head of [`exp428`](https://github.com/anatoli-starostin/spiky/tree/e85a5ceca156e79d199b0f6b395aa8e7e4ee287a/nanochat_exps/exp428_qkv_tph_x2). They looked far behind, but the runs were few and cut short, so we do not know whether tying is a dead end or a matter of a wider stream and larger coverage. Worth redoing properly.
- Magnitude-coded logits from a rank-coded stream, the problem as the report states it, is open.
