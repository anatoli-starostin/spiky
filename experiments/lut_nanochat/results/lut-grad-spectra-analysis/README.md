# Gradient geometry of LUT-FFN tables vs dense layers (analysis, no training)

Code: `experiments/lut_nanochat/analysis/lut_grad_spectra/` (README there). Full tables: `summary.md` (per layer, all
measurements), `results.csv` (per layer), `results.json` (everything compact). Plots: `spectra_lut_vs_dense.png`,
`hit_skew.png`, `hit_vs_gradnorm.png`, `redundancy_R.png`, `lookahead_curves.png`, `lookahead_best.png`, `grad_norms.png`.

## What was used
Checkpoints that exist (`results/*/checkpoints`): LUT nodropout1500 (steps 1000, 1500), LUT v2repro500 (250, 500), dense AdamW 2k (1500, 2000), dense AdamW continuation (5500, 5568). Used: **LUT 1500** (dropout 0, main), **LUT 500** (dropout 0.2, plus the same checkpoint with dropout off), **dense AdamW 2000** (reference), dense 5568 (optional, A/E only). Model built from the checkpoint meta exactly as base_train does (depth 24, fp8 tensorwise ON, LUT h16/d48/tph64/nap8/n=1, fused kernel); **no deviation from the runs except: eager instead of torch.compile**. Gradients: 8 micro-batches of 16x2048 (262k tokens, a quarter of the real 1M-token step), fixed seed, ClimbMix train data starting at the dense-2000 checkpoint's saved loader position (unseen by LUT 500/1500), identical batches for all models; D evaluated on 8 further held-out micro-batches (task asked 4; 8 for lower noise). Gradients are of the token loss; the cell-TV (lambda 10) part is reported separately/combined where stated. Everything is a single batch / single data position and 3 LUT variants: no error bars across data.

## Findings in short
1. **A. Output-side spectra.** The tables' 48-wide gradient spectrum is *more* lopsided than the dense one once you look where the structure lives: per table [256x48] top-1 energy share 0.70 (LUT 1500) / 0.85 (LUT 500) median, effective rank 2.9 / 1.8 of 48, stable rank 1.4 / 1.2; per head the same (0.71 / 0.85). Pooling the whole layer [262144x48] it is milder (top-1 0.30 / 0.36, eff. rank 12 / 7.8) because **each head has its own dominant 48-d direction**: |cos| between dominant directions of different tables of a head 0.70-0.97 (0.9 typical, 0.95+ at step 500), across heads 0.11-0.13 = random-48-d baseline 0.115. Dense (1536 wide): MLP top-1 share 0.21 median (0.13-0.5 for layers 0-19; 0.7-0.96 in layers 20-23), eff. rank 196 (of 1536, ~13%; collapses to 1-3 in the last 3 layers), sigma1/sigma_med 40; attention 0.39 / 86 / 203 (c_v and the last layers very lopsided, c_proj not). So dense gradients are flat-ish with a few dominant directions + long tail (sigma1/sigma_med 40-200), the table gradient is nearly rank-1-2 per table/head (sigma1/sigma_med 100-400 per table). Normalised, LUT whole-layer effective rank/48 ~0.25 vs dense MLP eff-rank/1536 ~0.13: not "more isotropic" than dense in either view. Step 500 (dropout on or off, same numbers) is more lopsided than step 1500. TV gradient alone has top-1 0.06-0.2, so it does not shape the spectrum. Signal/noise: |mean|^2/noise per 32k-token micro-batch 0.2-0.5 at step 1500 (0.2-0.8 at 500), i.e. ~1.7-2 for the 8-mb batch gradient; the noise covariance is itself lopsided (top-1 0.12-0.35).
2. **B. Cell side.** Hit counts are strongly skewed but not dead: per table (median over 24x1024 tables) Gini 0.57 (0.43-0.75 p10-p90), top-1% of cells take 9% and top-10% take 42% of hits, entropy 0.89 of max, zero-hit cells <0.4% (p90), min/median/max hits 16/544/9120 per 262k tokens (mean 1024; ~4x per real step); std of log hits 1.24. LUT 500: Gini 0.48, top-10% 34%. Skew is largest in the mid layers (Gini 0.59 at layer 6), a few tables have 0.8 zero cells. Cell gradient row norm follows hits closely: Spearman(hits, ||G_cell||) 0.87 median (0.79-0.93), 0.93 for score-mass. So (a) is real: the per-cell scale of G varies over ~1.5 decades (std log hits 1.2 -> factor ~3.5 per sigma, 1000x max/min); S^T S is diagonal so this is purely a per-row scale, which Adam-type elementwise normalisation (or row normalisation) already removes.
3. **C. Cross-table redundancy.** R (|sum dy_t|^2 / sum|dy_t|^2) is far from 64 (aligned) and close to the independent value 1 but above it: LUT 1500 median over layers 1.6 (SGD direction), 1.8 (Adam-normalised), 2.1 (row-normalised); range 1.4 (layer 6) to 3.3-5.3 (layer 23); layer 0 2.4. LUT 500 (dropout off): 3.2 / 4.0 / 4.7 (layer 0: 4-7). Mean cosine between table output contributions 0.03 (1500) / 0.09 (500); cosine between the gradients of different tables of a head ~0.01 signed, 0.04-0.06 absolute. So (c) is **weak at the batch-gradient level**: a joint step overshoots by a factor of ~2-5 in output-squared-norm, not 64, and it gets larger early in training and with normalised directions (normalising makes small contributions as large as big ones, so the alignment of the remainder dominates). It is a per-head-direction effect (the shared dominant 48-d direction), consistent with finding 1.
4. **D. One-step lookahead (proxy).** Held-out token-loss decrease at the best step (8 held-out mb; evaluation noise floor ~2e-4, fp8): LUT 1500: SGD 0.0012 (best rho=3e-3), per-layer-normalised SGD 0.0012; Adam-proxy 0.0035 (rho 0.3), Adam-sign 0.0040, row-norm 0.0025, whiten-layer 0.0029 (rho 0.03), whiten-head 0.0034, whiten-table 0.0031, whiten-layer o Adam-proxy 0.0037, row-norm o whiten-layer 0.0043 (rho 1), token-only Adam-proxy 0.0044. LUT 500 (dropout on): SGD 0.0023 (rho 0.03), Adam-proxy/sign 0.0068, row-norm 0.0045, whiten-layer 0.0047, whiten-head 0.0048, whiten-table 0.0046, row-norm o whiten-layer 0.0074, whiten-table o row-norm 0.0081 (rho 3); dropout-off numbers are the same within noise. Reading: (i) every elementwise/row/whitened preconditioner beats SGD by 2-4x and tolerates 30-300x larger relative steps (SGD blows up above rho~0.01-0.03; Adam-type up to ~0.3-1; whitened/row-normalised combos up to 1-3), (ii) row normalisation (cell side) and 48-wide whitening (output side) each recover a good part of the Adam-level gain (0.0025 / 0.0029 vs 0.0035-0.004 at step 1500), the combination row-norm o whiten reaches the best token-loss decrease at the largest step (0.0043 at 1500, 0.0074-0.0081 at 500, +10-20% over Adam), consistent with the two lopsidedness types (a) and (b) being roughly independent and both useful; whitening per head / per table is no better than per layer in token loss. (iii) Including the TV term the picture changes: the best *total* (token + TV) decrease is for plain 48-wide whitening (0.0034 at 1500, 0.0053 at 500) because TV grows when steps get large (TV0 = 0.010; at rho 1 it rises by several 0.1), so big Adam-type steps are limited by TV, not loss. (iv) per-layer-normalised steps change little vs global (cross-layer scale is not the issue). (v) Optimal rho for Adam is 0.3-1, i.e. the real lr (rho of a unit-rms update at lr 2.1e-3 = 0.4 at step 1500, 0.9 at 500) sits in the plateau; differences between the best directions are ~1-3e-3 on a 2.75 loss: **only a few times the noise floor, one batch, one step, no momentum: treat the ranking among the top 5-6 directions as unresolved**, the SGD-vs-rest gap as solid.
5. **E. Sanity / shares.** Fused backward == reference-ConfidenceLUT autograd to 3-5e-7 (relative, whole tensor) and matches central finite differences on single table entries to <2e-6 relative (fp32, real tables/inputs, layers 0/12/23). In the full fp8 model the directional derivative along the table gradient agrees with FD within 0.945-0.985 at rho 1e-3/1e-2 (off at 1e-4 where the forward noise dominates); single-entry FD in the full model is pure noise (fp8/bf16 forward noise ~1e-4 vs 4e-4 x eps signal), so that check is only meaningful in the cartridge-level test. Gradient norm share (squared batch-gradient norm): LUT 1500: tables 0.31% (token), compress 0.30%, decompress 0.30%, conf scalars 0.14%, attention 20%, other (embeddings/value-embeds/lm_head, scalars) 79%; LUT 500: tables 0.45%, attention 54%, other 45%. Dense: attention 46%, MLP 32%, other 22% (step 2000). The table gradient norm is 0.002-0.006 per layer vs 0.01-0.05 for attention; the TV gradient is another 0.0003-0.0006 per layer (5-30% of the token gradient norm). The tables carry a tiny part of the *gradient* energy but are updated by their own AdamW group, so this is only a statement about relevance for global clipping/scaling.

## Plain-language reading of H1
- (a) hit-frequency skew: **yes**, strongly (Gini ~0.57, top 10% of cells get ~42% of hits, ~1000x min/max), and gradient row norms follow it (Spearman ~0.87-0.93). Because S^T S is diagonal this is an exact per-row scale; row-/elementwise normalisation (what Adam already does) removes it. It does not need a matrix preconditioner.
- (b) output-side anisotropy: **yes, large but local**: each table/head's gradient is ~rank 1-3 in the 48-wide space (top-1 share 0.7-0.85); the dominant direction is shared by the 64 tables of a head (|cos| 0.9) but unrelated across heads. A single 48x48 whitening per layer therefore only fixes the part that survives pooling (top-1 0.3), a per-head 48x48 is the natural unit (spectrum/direction shared within the head); in the lookahead per-layer whitening was as good as per-head/per-table, so that distinction did not matter at one step. Dense matrices: lopsided mostly in the last layers and c_v; otherwise flat with a long tail; so the table spectrum is the *more* extreme one, in a 48-wide space.
- (c) redundancy: **present but mild** (R 1.6-5 instead of up to 64); it is the same per-head dominant direction seen from the token side, not a separate effect, and it is largest early in training, in layer 0 and the last layers.
- Where a Muon-like preconditioner could help: a combination of per-cell row scaling (cell side, removes (a)) and a 48x48 whitening shared by a head/layer (output side, removes (b)); SGD-style steps are the clear loser, Adam already gets most of the gain, the extra from also whitening is ~10-20% in the one-step proxy and below resolution of this analysis for finer choices. The TV term interacts: it caps usable step size.

## Caveats
Single batch/data position, one step (no momentum, no Adam state; "Adam-proxy" uses the 8 per-micro-batch second moments, which overestimate the real 1M-token second moment), fp8 evaluation noise ~2e-4 (comparable to the differences among the best directions), checkpoints at steps 500/1500 only (spectra change with training, 500 vs 1500 already differ), dense reference is AdamW-trained (not Muon-trained: its gradient spectra are those seen along an AdamW trajectory), whitening epsilon (1e-3 x mean eigenvalue) and the step grid are my choices, per-micro-batch gradients are 32k-token means. Per-layer numbers vary a lot between layers (see summary.md); medians hide the layer-0 and layers 20-23 differences. The 1/sqrt(64) scaling variant (6) was not run separately: with the relative-step (rho) grid any constant rescaling of a direction is absorbed into rho (the optimum rho of each direction is the answer to it).
